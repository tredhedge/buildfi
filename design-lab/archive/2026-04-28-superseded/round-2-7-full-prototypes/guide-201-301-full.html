01

Withdrawal order

The order you pull money out matters almost as much as how much you saved.

This chapter should feel like the real beginning of retirement strategy. The guide is no longer just explaining accounts; it is showing how account type changes the cost of each decision over time.

Reader takeaway

Taxable, RRSP/FERR, TFSA, and pensions are not just containers. They change how much after-tax flexibility you keep.

Guide role

The guide should give the framework. A future planner or report can personalize the exact draw order.

Web enhancement
Withdrawal-order comparison

An interactive version can compare two or three sequence patterns. The PDF should instead keep one worked example with a clear conclusion.

Report connection
Why this belongs near the report

02

Tax and OAS protection

Advanced readers do not need more noise. They need sharper threshold awareness.

The guide should frame tax not as a generic burden but as a sequence problem. Thresholds, clawbacks, and account choices all change the value of each next dollar of income.

Design principle: advanced content becomes more premium when it looks more deliberate, not more complicated.
OAS clawback

Readers need a clean mental model for why apparently efficient income can still become expensive after clawbacks.

Marginal reality

The guide can introduce effective tax rates, but it should stay interpretive rather than table-heavy.

03

Benefit timing

CPP/QPP and pension choices should be framed as tradeoffs in resilience, not just break-even math.

This is a good example of where the guide and the planner should differ. The guide explains the logic and the tradeoffs. The planner will eventually help choose an answer for a real household.

Fees and portfolio drag

The invisible cost deserves a chapter, not a footnote.

A stronger 201 guide can make fee drag feel like a strategic planning issue rather than an investing trivia point. That tone brings it closer to the AI report without making it fully client-personalized.

MER impact
