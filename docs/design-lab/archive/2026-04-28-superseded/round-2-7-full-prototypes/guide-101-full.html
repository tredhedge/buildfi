Part 101 · Foundations

Get your financial footing before you optimize anything.

This fuller prototype tests whether a beginner guide can feel more like a high-quality reading product without losing clarity. The aim is less app-page energy, more thoughtful chapter flow.

Start the guide Download PDF

Who this is for: readers who need a clear order of operations before diving into calculators. The guide should stand on its own, and interactive tools should deepen the lesson rather than carry it.

01

Your financial portrait

The first question is not how much you make. It is where you actually stand.

A fuller guide version should linger a bit more on interpretation. The reader needs to understand why net worth matters, why cash flow matters, and why trend matters more than the snapshot.

Core idea

Your net worth is the one number that captures both assets and obligations in the same frame.

Beginner value

Negative early-career net worth is not failure. The real question is whether the direction is improving each year.

Interactive enhancement
Net worth snapshot

In the online version, this chapter would let the reader enter a few balances and immediately see assets, liabilities, and net worth on one simple card.

PDF fallback
Static worked example

The PDF counterpart can show a single example household and how its net worth changes after one year of debt reduction and savings.

02

Budgeting for control

Budgeting should feel like permission with structure, not like shame with categories.

One thing the fuller guide can do better is frame the budget emotionally. The reader should leave this chapter feeling more agency, not more guilt.

A good 101 guide does not just say “50/30/20.” It explains what problem that structure solves for a real person.
Needs

Anchor essentials first so the reader knows what must be protected every month.

Flex

Show that discretionary spending is not the enemy; unmanaged drift is.

03

Debt before ambition

For many households, the first true investment is eliminating a painful interest rate.

This chapter should feel like the hinge between reading and future product use. The guide teaches the principle. The debt tool later helps the reader apply it with their own balances.

Online guide moment
Card-trap calculator

The HTML guide can embed a mini illustration showing how a 19.99% card stretches out over years under minimum payments.

Tool handoff
Go deeper in debt tool

After the principle lands, the guide can invite the reader into the dedicated debt tool to compare strategies with their own numbers.

04

Savings order

Each extra dollar needs a sequence, not just a destination.

This chapter is where the guide becomes visibly useful. Readers want to know whether to build cash, take the employer match, kill debt, or fund tax-advantaged accounts.

Emergency reserve

Stability before optimization keeps the reader from falling back into expensive credit.

Employer match

The guide should explain why “free money” outranks almost everything else.

Tax shelter

Only after the basics are stable does the account-choice question really matter.

05

Accounts and public benefits

Readers need plain-language meaning, not a wall of acronyms.

The fuller guide should connect the accounts to future life choices. RRSP, TFSA, FHSA, RRQ/QPP, OAS, and GIS matter because they change later flexibility, not because they are technical categories.

Static explanation
Account comparison table

The PDF should keep a clean comparison table. The web guide can add revealable notes or a tiny chooser for “income high now vs later.”

Future bridge
Lead into Guide 201

The right ending for 101 is not “now you know everything.” It is “now you are ready for optimization and timing questions.”

What happens next

Guide first. Tool second. Plan later.

This is the intended portfolio logic: the beginner guide clarifies the mental model, the tool supports action, and the planner or report comes later when the person is ready for a deeper structured plan.
