01

Respect the bracket bands

Advanced does not have to mean cluttered.

The current opportunity is to make 201 feel more like a strategic memo. The page should still breathe, even when the ideas are more technical. That is what connects it naturally to the AI report language.

Reader takeaway

Tax thresholds are not just technical boundaries. They change how expensive each extra withdrawal year becomes.

Design takeaway

The page should communicate that insight with calm hierarchy, not with more boxes and controls.

02

Sequence the accounts deliberately

The order matters almost as much as the assets themselves.

This is where 201 can start to feel like a report chapter. The shell should carry more seriousness than 101, and the prose can become more strategic, but the reading path should remain linear and coherent.

Reportward principle: the guide should sound like it is preparing the reader for a planning conversation, not just teaching a concept in isolation.
03

Delay or claim benefits with intent

This is where timing becomes a design problem, not only a math problem.

Advanced readers will accept more depth, but they still benefit from calm sequencing. Benefit timing should appear as a structured choice with explicit tradeoffs, not as an early “bonus simulator” teaser.

Earlier claim

More cash flow now, but lower protected income later.

Delayed claim

Stronger later-life floor, but higher demand on bridge assets.

Bridge to the product

Only now invite simulation.

The right handoff comes after the guide has taught the framework. That preserves the publication tone while still supporting the product ecosystem.
