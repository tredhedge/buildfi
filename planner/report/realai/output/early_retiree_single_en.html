Strategic synthesis

The plan's main levers — tax (effective rate 19%), withdrawal sequencing, estate — do not read independently. Withdrawal-order choice changes the lifetime tax rate, which changes the residual estate.

The trajectory requires a recovery sequence, not a failure diagnosis: stabilize cash flow, deleverage high-rate debt, rebuild savings capacity, then re-plan goals. Each phase prepares the next.

Among the levers tested, projected inflation move the success rate by about ±23 pts, versus ±18 pts for returns. Return-assumption sensitivity dominates; the other factors trail behind.
Recovery sequence (38% baseline success)

At this success level, the trajectory needs a phased recovery rather than a single adjustment. Each phase prepares the next.

  1. 1. Stabilize cash flow. Phase one: a balanced monthly budget closes the current $10K/mo gap. Without this stabilization, the other adjustments do not hold.
  2. 2. Expand savings capacity. Phase two: without high-rate debt, the priority is widening the monthly margin available for savings — reduce discretionary spending or raise income.
  3. 3. Allocate recovered savings. Phase three: direct the new dollars to the account that maximizes after-tax return based on your marginal bracket (TFSA-first to cap, then RRSP).
  4. 4. Re-plan goals. Phase four: as monthly savings grow, the key parameters (target age, target spending, profile) are reassessed against the new trajectory.

16Methodology

In plain terms
We tested your plan against 5000 simulated futures, including the worst-case 5% by design (2008 crash, persistent inflation, long life). The success rate is the share of those futures where your savings hold to the end of the modeled horizon. Figures above are conditional and rest on the assumptions detailed below.
This projection uses 5000 independent Monte Carlo simulations. Each simulation generates random trajectories for returns (equities and bonds), inflation, and mortality, then calculates year-by-year wealth evolution applying tax rules, withdrawal strategies, and government benefit calculations.
Unlike a deterministic projection — which assumes one fixed return every year and produces a single number, hiding the sequence-of-returns risk that matters most in retirement — this simulation approach yields a range of outcomes and a success rate rather than false precision. Returns are modeled with fat tails (Student-t, df = 5) to represent rare but real market crashes; this choice, applied by default, produces more conservative success rates than a normal-distribution model, with the difference concentrated in the adverse scenarios (resilience, depletion).
Market assumptions: Equity return 7.0% / bonds 3.5%. fat tails (rare crashes more likely than under a normal distribution). Cross-asset correlation across 5 classes (DMS 2024). Stochastic inflation ±1.5%.
Mortality: CPM-2023 tables (CIA). Deterministic horizon: 95 yrs. CPM life expectancy: M=85.5, F=87.4 (at 65).
Taxation: Federal and provincial 2026 brackets indexed. Pension credit ($2,000), age credit ($8,790), OAS 75+ bonus (10%). Capital gains inclusion: 50% / 67%.
Advanced parameters: MER RRSP 0.4%, TFSA 0.4%, NR 0.4%. Equity alloc: RRSP 75%, TFSA 75%, NR 65%. Spending curve (Go/Slow/No): 100/90/75%.

18Glossary

Definitions of technical terms used in this report. Terms with a dotted underline throughout the report open a tooltip on hover.
Age credit
Non-refundable credit for those 65+. Phased out progressively above an income threshold (federal $44,325 in 2026), eliminated much higher.
AMF (Autorité des marchés financiers)
Quebec financial regulator. Oversees financial security advisors, financial planners, and compliance of the language used in financial reports.
Asset allocation
Split between equities, bonds and other classes. Simultaneously determines expected return and portfolio volatility.
Bridge income
Temporary income (often from a DB plan or private annuity) paid between an early retirement and the age of eligibility for public benefits (typically 60-65).
CCA (Capital Cost Allowance)
Tax depreciation of a rental property (typically 4% declining balance for Class 1). Reduces taxable income each year but triggers "recapture" on sale.
CCA recapture
On sale of a rental property, all CCA previously claimed must be added back to taxable income that year — at 100% inclusion, full marginal rate, not as capital gain.
CCPC (Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation)
Active corporation eligible for the small business deduction (SBD). Salary/dividend mix adjustable based on corporate and personal income.
CDA (Capital Dividend Account)
Corporate account accumulating the non-taxable portion of capital gains (50%). Distributable to the owner tax-free as a capital dividend.
CESG (Canada Education Savings Grant)
Federal grant equal to 20% of RESP contributions (max $500/year, $7,200 lifetime per child). Deposited directly into the RESP.
Coverage ratio (guaranteed income)
Share of target spending covered by lifetime guaranteed income (CPP/QPP + OAS + DB pensions). The higher the ratio, the less the plan relies on markets.
CPP / QPP
Canada Pension Plan or Quebec Pension Plan. Indexed monthly pension for life. Flexible start age 60-70 (7.2%/yr reduction before 65, 8.4%/yr boost after).
CPP/QPP benefit sharing
Distinct from pension splitting: a couple can request that CPP/QPP benefits be shared between spouses on their tax returns. Permanent and formal.
Defined-benefit (DB) pension
Pension plan where the pension amount is defined in advance (formula over years of service × average salary). Actuarial risk stays with the employer.
Defined-contribution (DC) pension
Plan where employer and employee contribute defined amounts; the final pension depends on accumulated returns. Market risk stays with the employee.
Dividend gross-up
Tax markup applied to dividends received when computing taxable income (38% eligible, 15% non-eligible). Inflates net income — can trigger OAS clawback even without extra real tax.
Drawdown
Phase where accumulated savings fund expenses. Withdrawal order (RRSP, TFSA, non-registered) influences total lifetime tax paid.
Effective marginal rate
Real rate paid on the next dollar of income, including clawbacks (OAS, GIS, credits). Can exceed 70% in certain ranges — well above the nominal marginal rate.
Effective tax rate
Total tax paid divided by taxable income, in %. Different from marginal rate (which applies to the next dollar earned).
Eligible dividend
Dividend paid from corporate income taxed at the general (non-SBD) rate. Receives a higher gross-up and a more generous tax credit than non-eligible dividends.
Estate (net inheritance)
Wealth remaining at death, after taxes due at that point (notably on RRSP/RRIF balances). Includes residence, investments and debts.
Fat-tailed distribution
Statistical model giving more probability to extreme events (crashes, exceptional years) than a normal distribution. More realistic for stock returns.
FHSA (First Home Savings Account)
Combines RRSP-style tax deduction with TFSA-style tax-free withdrawals, for a first home purchase. $8,000/year, $40,000 lifetime.
GIS (Guaranteed Income Supplement)
Federal benefit for low-income retirees (on top of OAS). Clawed back at 50¢ per dollar of non-OAS income — a significant tax trap.
Glide path
Gradual reduction of equity allocation as retirement approaches, and during retirement. Reduces volatility at the point a market drop would hurt most.
Inflation
General rise in cost of living. Reduces savings purchasing power. Model assumption: ~2.1% per year (Bank of Canada long-term).
Initial withdrawal rate
Percentage of capital withdrawn in the first year of retirement. Classic "4% rule", to be moderated by age, longevity and risk tolerance.
Integrated rate
Combined corporate + shareholder tax rate on a dollar earned by the corp and then distributed. Measures whether income is better extracted as salary or dividend.
IPP (Individual Pension Plan)
DB-style pension plan for a corporate shareholder or senior executive. Higher deductible contributions than an RRSP after age 50.
LCGE (Lifetime Capital Gains Exemption)
Roughly $1.25M lifetime exemption on the sale of qualified small business corporation (QSBC) shares or qualified farm/fishing property. Holding-period rules apply.
LIF (Life Income Fund)
Drawdown account created from a LIRA. Withdrawals bounded by a minimum (like a RRIF) AND a maximum (unlike a RRIF).
LIRA (Locked-In Retirement Account)
Account created when an employee leaves a pension plan and transfers accrued benefits. Withdrawal rules more restrictive than an RRSP.
Longevity (CPM-2023)
Canadian projected life expectancy tables published by the Canadian Institute of Actuaries in 2023. Account for future mortality improvements.
MER (Management Expense Ratio)
Annual fund fee, expressed as % of assets. Directly reduces net return. See the fee impact table by placement type.
Monte Carlo
Simulation method that replays the plan thousands of times with randomized but realistic returns and inflation, to measure robustness against uncertainty.
Nominal dollars
Amounts in the dollars of the year they occur (with inflation). $100,000 today is much larger in nominal dollars 30 years out.
Nominal marginal rate
Federal + provincial tax rate applied to the next dollar earned, by bracket. Excludes clawbacks on government benefits.
Non-eligible dividend
Dividend paid from corporate income taxed at the small-business rate. Lower gross-up and tax credit than eligible dividends.
OAS (Old Age Security)
Universal federal pension from age 65 (deferral possible to 70, +0.6%/month). Progressive clawback if net taxable income exceeds $95,323 (2026 threshold).
OAS clawback
Progressive reduction of OAS above the income threshold (~$95K in 2026), at 15¢ per excess dollar. Fully phased out around $154K.
P25 — Cautious scenario
25% of Monte Carlo simulations end below this level, 75% above. Represents an unfavourable but plausible outcome.
P5 — Adverse scenario
5% of Monte Carlo simulations end below this level. Represents the adverse tail of the distribution — stress case, not base case.
P50 — Median
The central trajectory: as many simulations end above as below. Often called the "typical scenario".
P75 — Favourable scenario
75% of simulations end below this level, 25% above. Represents a better-than-average outcome.
P95 — Very favourable scenario
95% of simulations end below this level, 5% above. Represents an exceptionally good outcome — useful for estate sizing, not for routine planning.
Pension income credit
15% federal credit on the first $2,000 of eligible pension income (RRIF, DB annuity) starting at age 65. Provincial credit stacks (~$300 savings in Quebec).
Pension income splitting
Tax mechanism allowing up to 50% of certain pension income to be transferred to a spouse for tax purposes, to reduce the couple's combined tax.
RDTOH (Refundable Dividend Tax on Hand)
Corporate tax account accumulating refundable tax on passive investment income. The corporation recovers $1 of RDTOH per $2.61 of eligible dividend paid.
Real dollars
Amounts expressed in constant purchasing power (inflation removed). Allows comparison across years without money illusion.
RESP (Registered Education Savings Plan)
Savings account for a child's post-secondary education. Receives the CESG (20% federal grant, $7,200/child lifetime cap) and in Quebec the QESI.
Return variance
Measure of how simulated returns spread around their mean. High variance = widely spread possible paths = more uncertainty about the final outcome.
RRIF (Registered Retirement Income Fund)
Mandatory drawdown account after the RRSP (no later than age 71). Annual minimum withdrawals prescribed by age.
RRSP (Registered Retirement Savings Plan)
Retirement savings account with tax deduction on contributions and tax on withdrawals. Mandatory conversion to RRIF by age 71.
RRSP meltdown
Strategy of voluntary accelerated RRSP withdrawals before 72 to smooth taxes and reduce the balance converted to RRIF (and therefore mandatory minimum withdrawals later).
Savings depletion
Moment when capital reaches zero before projected death. The plan then relies solely on public benefits (CPP/QPP, OAS, GIS).
SBD (Small Business Deduction)
Reduces corporate tax rate on the first $500,000 of active income for a CCPC. Ground down if passive investment income exceeds $50,000/yr, eliminated at $150,000.
Sequence-of-returns risk
Risk that a market drop occurs early in retirement, when withdrawals crystallize losses. The first years matter more than the last.
Smith Manoeuvre
Gradual conversion of a non-deductible mortgage into deductible interest: each payment frees HELOC room re-borrowed to invest. Carries real leverage exposure.
Stochastic mortality
Rather than a fixed death age, the engine draws the death age from the CPM-2023 table on each simulation. Captures the real uncertainty about lifespan.
Success rate
Percentage of Monte Carlo simulations where savings do not run out before projected death age. 90%+ = very solid, 60-89% = monitor closely, under 60% = adjustments needed.
TFSA (Tax-Free Savings Account)
Savings account where gains and withdrawals are tax-free. Contributions are not deductible. Cumulative room: up to $109,000 in 2026 for those who turned 18 in 2009 (lower otherwise).
VaR 5% (worst-percentile expected loss)
Wealth at the 5th percentile of Monte Carlo simulations: 95% of scenarios do better, 5% do worse. A tail-risk measure, not a central scenario.
Withdrawal cushion
2 to 3 years of spending held in cash or short bonds. Avoids forced equity sales during a drawdown — direct protection against sequence-of-returns risk.
