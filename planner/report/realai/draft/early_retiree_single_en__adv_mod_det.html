Opening letter

Jordan, retiring at 55 is a deliberate choice — one that compresses a working life into a single decisive window and stretches the resulting savings across roughly four decades of withdrawals. That asymmetry is what makes a FIRE plan structurally different from a conventional retirement at 65: every dollar must work harder, every early-market downturn lands with more weight, and every adjustment made in the bridge years before public benefits arrive carries outsized influence on the rest of the horizon. This report is built around that reality. It examines the 5 years separating you from your target retirement age, the sequence-of-returns window that opens the moment paycheques stop, and the levers that could meaningfully shift the balance — withdrawal pacing, the gap between 55 and the age public benefits begin, and how the OAS deferral to 70 reshapes income later. The intent is to make trade-offs visible, not to prescribe a path. The conclusions you read here would benefit from a conversation that accounts for your full personal context. Consult a certified financial planner before acting on any element.

The dominant lever of your plan: the 10-year bridge between retirement at age 55 and the start of CPP/OAS at 65. This is the window most exposed to sequence-of-returns risk ; a dedicated liquid reserve (bond ladder, reserve fund) isolates the plan from an early market drop. The early vs deferred CPP/OAS trade-off is central : starting early (CPP at 60, OAS at 65) covers the bridge with guaranteed income ; defer CPP and OAS to age 70 adds up to +42% lifetime CPP and +36% lifetime OAS at the cost of heavier portfolio draws during the bridge.

16Methodology

In plain terms
We tested your plan against 5000 simulated futures, including the worst-case 5% by design (2008 crash, persistent inflation, long life). The success rate is the share of those futures where your savings hold to the end of the modeled horizon. Figures above are conditional and rest on the assumptions detailed below.

18Glossary

Definitions of technical terms used in this report. Terms with a dotted underline throughout the report open a tooltip on hover.
Asset allocation
Split between equities, bonds and other classes. Simultaneously determines expected return and portfolio volatility.
CCPC (Canadian-Controlled Private Corporation)
Active corporation eligible for the small business deduction (SBD). Salary/dividend mix optimizable based on corporate and personal income.
CPP / QPP
Canada Pension Plan or Quebec Pension Plan. Indexed monthly pension for life. Flexible start age 60-70 (7.2%/yr reduction before 65, 8.4%/yr boost after).
Drawdown
Phase where accumulated savings fund expenses. Withdrawal order (RRSP, TFSA, non-registered) influences total lifetime tax paid.
Effective tax rate
Total tax paid divided by taxable income, in %. Different from marginal rate (which applies to the next dollar earned).
Estate (net inheritance)
Wealth remaining at death, after taxes due at that point (notably on RRSP/RRIF balances). Includes residence, investments and debts.
Fat-tailed distribution (t-Student)
Statistical model giving more probability to extreme events (crashes, exceptional years) than a normal distribution. More realistic for stock returns.
GIS (Guaranteed Income Supplement)
Federal benefit for low-income retirees (on top of OAS). Clawed back at 50¢ per dollar of non-OAS income — a significant tax trap.
Inflation
General rise in cost of living. Reduces savings purchasing power. Model assumption: ~2.1% per year (Bank of Canada long-term).
Initial withdrawal rate
Percentage of capital withdrawn in the first year of retirement. Classic "4% rule", to be moderated by age, longevity and risk tolerance.
Integrated rate
Combined corporate + shareholder tax rate on a dollar earned by the corp and then distributed. Measures whether income is better extracted as salary or dividend.
LIRA (Locked-In Retirement Account)
Account created when an employee leaves a pension plan and transfers accrued benefits. Withdrawal rules more restrictive than an RRSP.
Longevity (CPM-2023)
Canadian projected life expectancy tables published by the Canadian Institute of Actuaries in 2023. Account for future mortality improvements.
MER (Management Expense Ratio)
Annual fund fee, expressed as % of assets. Directly reduces net return. See the fee impact table by placement type.
Monte Carlo
Simulation method that replays the plan thousands of times with randomized but realistic returns and inflation, to measure robustness against uncertainty.
Nominal dollars
Amounts in the dollars of the year they occur (with inflation). $100,000 today is much larger in nominal dollars 30 years out.
OAS (Old Age Security)
Universal federal pension from age 65 (deferral possible to 70, +0.6%/month). Progressive clawback if net taxable income exceeds $95,323 (2026 threshold).
OAS clawback
Progressive reduction of OAS above the income threshold (~$95K in 2026), at 15¢ per excess dollar. Fully phased out around $154K.
P25 — Cautious scenario
25% of Monte Carlo simulations end below this level, 75% above. Represents an unfavourable but plausible outcome.
P50 — Median
The central trajectory: as many simulations end above as below. Often called the "typical scenario".
P75 — Favourable scenario
75% of simulations end below this level, 25% above. Represents a better-than-average outcome.
Pension income splitting
Tax mechanism allowing up to 50% of certain pension income to be transferred to a spouse for tax purposes, to reduce the couple's combined tax.
Real dollars
Amounts expressed in constant purchasing power (inflation removed). Allows comparison across years without money illusion.
RRIF (Registered Retirement Income Fund)
Mandatory drawdown account after the RRSP (no later than age 71). Annual minimum withdrawals prescribed by age.
RRSP (Registered Retirement Savings Plan)
Retirement savings account with tax deduction on contributions and tax on withdrawals. Mandatory conversion to RRIF by age 71.
RRSP meltdown
Strategy of voluntary accelerated RRSP withdrawals before 72 to smooth taxes and reduce the balance converted to RRIF (and therefore mandatory minimum withdrawals later).
Savings depletion
Moment when capital reaches zero before projected death. The plan then relies solely on public benefits (CPP/QPP, OAS, GIS).
Sequence-of-returns risk
Risk that a market drop occurs early in retirement, when withdrawals crystallize losses. The first years matter more than the last.
Success rate
Percentage of Monte Carlo simulations where savings do not run out before projected death age. 90%+ = very solid, 60-89% = monitor closely, under 60% = adjustments needed.
TFSA (Tax-Free Savings Account)
Savings account where gains and withdrawals are tax-free. Contributions are not deductible. Cumulative limit: ~$95,000 in 2026 for those 18+ since 2009.
